# Mode · outreach

Writing to a person, and following up. See
[JOB_SEARCH_PLAYBOOK.md](../docs/JOB_SEARCH_PLAYBOOK.md) for where the people are.

## The hard rule, before anything else

**You send it. Always.** This mode produces a draft in a file. It never sends, connects,
comments, or messages. There is no flag that changes this.

## Who is worth writing to

In rough order of how likely a reply is:

1. Someone who has already worked with you
2. Someone who announced the opening themselves
3. An alumnus of somewhere you studied
4. Someone whose profile says they mentor
5. Someone whose work genuinely overlaps yours
6. The named contact on the advert

**Not** several people at one employer about one role, and not a recruiter and a hiring manager
in the same week.

## The draft

Four or five sentences. Who you are in one clause. What you are interested in and why it is
them. One small question. Stop.

**No attachment. No CV. No application.** A first message that carries a file is a request, not
a conversation.

## Timing

**Tie the send to something.** After you apply, after they post, after the event you both
attended. An untethered message has no reason to exist today rather than any other day.

**Every draft gets a send-by date.** A note written for a moment goes stale when the moment
passes. See [G-13](../lessons/G-13-status-comes-from-the-record.md).

## Following up

**One follow-up**, after one to two weeks, adding something rather than repeating. A second is
not persistence.

For an application with no response: one polite note at the point the advert said they would
decide, or after three weeks if it said nothing.

**Record every send and every silence.** Silence is data; it is how you learn which channels
work for you rather than which ones are supposed to.

## Recording it

Per person: who, where, how you found them, what you sent, when, and what came back.
**Including nothing.**
