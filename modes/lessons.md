# Mode · lessons

How a mistake becomes a rule.

## When something becomes a lesson

**Not every mistake.** A lesson is worth writing when it would happen again, to you, in a
situation you cannot yet see coming. A one-off does not need a rule; it needs fixing.

**Two signals it is real:** you have made it twice, or it cost you something you cannot get
back.

## The shape

    # G-NN · The rule, as an imperative

    **One sentence stating the rule.**

    ## What happened
    Briefly. One example, not three.

    ## Why it works
    The mechanism, so it transfers to a case that looks different.

    ## The check
    The specific thing to do, concretely enough to be a step.

## Rules for writing one

**State it so it holds outside your own field.** If it only makes sense for your profession,
it is a domain fact and belongs in your profile, not the lesson set.

**One example, occasionally two.** The example is there to make the rule memorable, not to
prove it. Three examples means the rule is not stated clearly enough yet.

**Name the failure, not the fix.** "A negative result is a fact about your method" travels.
"Always add a positive control" does not, because you will not recognise when it applies.

**Cross-reference rather than repeat.** Two lessons saying the same thing means one lesson.

## Maintaining the set

**Merge lessons that turn out to be the same rule.** This happens more than you expect, and a
set that only grows stops being read.

**Retire the ones that stop being true.** Mark them retired with the date rather than deleting,
so you do not re-derive them.

**Measure before changing one.** [G-09](../lessons/G-09-measure-a-rule-change-first.md).

**If nobody reads them, there are too many.** Under twenty is the target.
