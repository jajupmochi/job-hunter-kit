# Mode · evaluate

Decide whether a role is worth your time, **before** any writing happens.

## Order matters

Check eligibility first. **The commonest waste in a job search is a well-written application to
a role that was never open to you.**

### 1. Eligibility, first and always

Read the whole advert, especially the bottom. Citizenship, work authorisation, clearance,
time-since-graduation windows, on-site requirements. See
[G-12](../lessons/G-12-read-the-eligibility-clause-first.md).

**If a clause rules you out, stop here and record why**, so the same role does not come back
next month and get re-evaluated from scratch.

### 2. Liveness

Confirmed open at the employer's own source today, or it does not get written for.

### 3. Fit, in two parts

**What they asked for**, point by point, with your evidence against each. Gaps included.

**What the work actually is**, which is often not what the title says. Read the
responsibilities rather than the headline.

### 4. Terms

Contract length, start date, location, and anything that would make you decline an offer.
**Check this before writing, not after an interview.**

### 5. The verdict

One of: **apply now** with a date, **apply if time allows**, **not this one** with a reason, or
**needs a question answered first** with the question.

**Record the verdict even when it is no.** A decision that lives only in your head gets made
again, and next time you might get it wrong.

## Due diligence, proportional

For anything you would actually take: who they are, whether they are hiring or churning, who
the team is, and whether anything about the posting is off. **A role that has been reposted
every quarter for two years is telling you something.**
