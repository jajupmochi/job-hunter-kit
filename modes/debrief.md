# Mode · debrief

**After anything happens.** An interview, a rejection, a reply, a call, an offer, or a silence
that has gone on long enough to mean something.

This is the mode that makes the kit compound instead of merely automating. **Everything in
[`lessons/`](../lessons/) started as a debrief.**

## When to run it

Same day, while you still remember. **Recall degrades faster than anyone expects**, and a
debrief written three days later is a reconstruction.

## The questions

Answer in your own words, badly, quickly. Polish is not the point.

**1. What actually happened?** The sequence. Who said what, in what order. Separate what you
heard from what you inferred, and mark the inferences.

**2. What surprised you?** The most valuable line in any debrief. A surprise is a wrong model,
and the wrong model is still in your head unless you write it down.

**3. What did you get wrong?** Not to punish yourself. The specific thing: an answer that
landed badly, a question you did not ask, an assumption about the role that was false.

**4. What went better than you thought?** People systematically miscalibrate this in one
direction, and it changes what you emphasise next time.

**5. What would you do differently?** One sentence, and specific enough to act on.

**6. Is there a rule here?** Most of the time, no. Occasionally yes, and then it goes to
[`lessons.md`](lessons.md).

## For a rejection specifically

**Separate what you can learn from what you cannot.** Most rejections carry no information:
someone internal, someone with an exact-match background, a role that changed. Reading a
message into those is a way of hurting yourself for nothing.

**But check three things.** Was there an eligibility clause you missed? Did you apply late? Was
this outside what you said you were looking for?

**And ask whether the person stays.** A closed role is not a closed relationship. The people
who rejected you know your work now, and some of them will hire again.

## Honesty rules

**Write what happened, not what you wish had happened.** A debrief you have flattered is worse
than none, because you will act on it.

**Mark inference as inference.** "They seemed unconvinced" is a guess. "They asked the same
question twice" is an observation. Keep them apart, because the observation stays true and the
guess often does not.

**If someone corrects your account later, rewrite the record**, and note what you had wrong.
The correction is usually more instructive than the original.

## Output

One file per event, next to the application it belongs to. Short. Then, if a rule fell out of
it, [`lessons.md`](lessons.md).
