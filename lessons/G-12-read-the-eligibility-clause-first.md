# G-12 · A working link is not proof you may apply

**Read the whole advert for eligibility before you invest anything in it.** The clause that
disqualifies you is usually one line, usually near the bottom, and usually not in the
requirements section where you would look for it.

## What to look for, every time

- **Citizenship or residency.** "Open to citizens of X only." Common in government, defence,
  national laboratories and some publicly funded research.
- **Work authorisation.** Whether they sponsor, and whether they say so.
- **Security clearance**, including "must be eligible for", which is a citizenship rule in
  other words.
- **Time windows.** "Within N years of graduation", "no more than N years of experience".
  These exclude people who are more than qualified.
- **Location.** On-site requirements, relocation expectations, time-zone overlap.
- **Internal-only** postings, and postings advertised for legal reasons with a candidate
  already chosen.

## Why this keeps happening

**The link works, the page loads, the requirements match, and everything looks fine.** Nothing
about a page that renders tells you whether you are allowed to apply. Automated extraction
makes it worse: a truncated read stops before the bottom of the page, which is exactly where
the clause lives.

## When you find one

**Re-check the whole batch.** If one advert had a clause you missed, the others were read the
same way. One missed clause is a mistake; the same mistake across a list is a method.
