# G-07 · Every claim traces to a source, or it is marked unknown

**The single most important rule in this kit.** One invented detail, caught, ends the
application and possibly the relationship. There is no upside that justifies the risk.

## Three inflations that happen without anyone deciding to lie

| Inflation | What it looks like | The honest version |
|---|---|---|
| **Role** | "I led" when you contributed | "I was one of the people who built" |
| **Status** | "deployed", "in production" for something built and shelved | "a working prototype", "in internal testing" |
| **Ownership** | claiming work you supervised, or a group's work as yours | "our team's", and say which part was yours |

Each is a small step from the truth taken to make a sentence stronger. **Together they are how
a CV drifts away from a life** without anyone noticing.

## Numbers are re-checked, never remembered

Every number goes stale: how many users, how many stars, how many people, how long. **Look it
up the day you send it.** A number that was true last year and is wrong today reads exactly
like a number you made up.

## When you do not know

Write `<TBD>` and leave it. **A draft with a visible hole is safe. A draft with a plausible
guess in the hole is a lie waiting to be discovered**, because by the time anyone reads it,
nobody remembers which parts were guesses.

## The dividing question

Before any factual sentence: *where would I point if they asked me to prove this?* If the
answer is a document, a URL, a record or a person, it is a claim. If the answer is "it feels
about right", it is not.
