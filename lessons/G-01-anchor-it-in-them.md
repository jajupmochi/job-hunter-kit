# G-01 · Anchor it in them

**Paragraph one names something only this employer would recognise.**

Not the industry, not the mission statement, not something true of every organisation of that
type. A named project, a named product, a specific method they use, a decision they published,
a number from their own annual report. Something you could only write after reading about them.

## The check: swap their name out

Replace the organisation's name with a competitor's. **If the letter still reads correctly, it
is a template**, and the reader will feel that even if they cannot say why. Rewrite until the
swap breaks it.

This test is unforgiving and takes ten seconds. Use it on every letter before it goes out.

## Worked example

*Fails the swap.* "I am impressed by your commitment to innovation and your collaborative
culture." True of anyone. Swap the name, nothing breaks.

*Passes.* "Your team's decision to keep the scheduling engine in-process rather than split it
out is the opposite of what most groups your size do, and the reasoning in your engineering
post is why I want to work on it." Swap the name and the sentence becomes false.

## Why it works

An application is read by someone who has read forty others that week. Almost all of them open
with a sentence that could have been sent to anybody. **The one that could not is the one that
gets read to the end.**
