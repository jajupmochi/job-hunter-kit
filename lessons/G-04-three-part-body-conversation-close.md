# G-04 · Three parts, then ask for a conversation

A body that works for almost any application, in almost any field.

## The three parts

**1. Why them.** What this organisation does that you specifically want to be part of. Passes
the swap test from [G-01](G-01-anchor-it-in-them.md).

**2. Why you, point by point against what they asked for.** Take their requirements in their
order and answer each with evidence. Not a summary of your CV. **A reader with a checklist is
looking for their own words back**, and giving them that costs you nothing.

**3. The shared problem.** One paragraph about a difficulty in the work itself, and how you
would approach it. This is the paragraph that separates a good application from a competent
one, because it shows you thought about the job rather than about getting the job.

## The close: ask for a conversation, not a decision

**"I would welcome a short conversation about X" is easier to say yes to than "I hope to hear
from you regarding my application."** The first asks for twenty minutes. The second asks them
to decide your candidacy. People say yes to small asks.

Name what the conversation would be about. A specific subject makes it a meeting rather than
an obligation.

## What to leave out

No summary paragraph. No restating what you already said. **If you find yourself writing "In
conclusion" or "To summarise", the letter is already too long** and the summary is the part
to cut, not the part to add.
