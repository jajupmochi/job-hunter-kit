# Where to look, and how often

> Every example in this kit is invented. See the disclaimer in the [README](../README.md).

This describes **channels and cadence**. It deliberately contains no target list, no country
ranking and no field assumptions, because those are yours and only you can set them.

## Decide four things first, and write them down

Everything downstream depends on these, and none of them can be defaulted for you.

1. **Where you already have the right to work**, and where a permit is realistically obtainable
   for someone in your position. This is a fact, not a preference, and it outranks every
   preference.
2. **What you would move for, and on what timeline.** A role that requires relocating in six
   weeks is a different role from the same job starting in six months.
3. **The shortest contract worth taking**, per location. A one-year contract can be a sensible
   way into a country with a route to permanence, and a bad trade into one without.
4. **What you will not apply for.** The exclusion list matters as much as the inclusion list,
   because it is what stops a search drifting into whatever happens to be posted.

Re-read this note monthly. It changes more than you expect, and a stale answer here quietly
misdirects every sweep after it.

## The four channel families

**Rotate across families.** No single channel should supply a whole session, because each one
has a blind spot the others do not.

### A. Personalised recommendations

The platform's own suggestions for you, on the platforms where you have a profile. **Cheap,
and nobody else's search reproduces them**, because they are computed from your history. Often
the highest-yield channel per minute spent, and the one most often skipped.

### B. Employer boards and applicant tracking systems

When you know an employer's name, **their own board is authoritative**. Aggregators show
postings that closed weeks ago; an employer's list of what is open cannot. Most of these
systems expose structured data, which makes them reliable to read. See
[data/platforms.yml](../data/platforms.yml).

### C. Aggregators and alerts

Broad coverage, more filtering. Set up saved searches and let them run rather than browsing.
**An alert you tuned once and receive daily beats a search you run when you remember to.**

### D. People

Announcements from the people doing the hiring, professional communities, mailing lists,
alumni networks, former colleagues. **This is the channel where a posting comes with a human
attached**, which no board listing does. See [JOB_SEARCH_PLAYBOOK.md](JOB_SEARCH_PLAYBOOK.md).

## Cadence

| Rhythm | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Daily, short** | Read the alerts. Act only on the ones with a deadline or an obvious fit. |
| **Weekly, longer** | One rotation through the four families. Verify anything you plan to apply to. Update the record. |
| **Monthly** | Re-read your four decisions above. Re-check whether your channels still pay for themselves; retire the ones that have produced nothing in two months. |

## Two axes, not one

Most people search on **domain** alone: the subject their last job was about. That is half a
search.

The other axis is **method**: the transferable thing you are good at, independent of subject.
A person who forecasts demand for a retailer and a person who forecasts river levels are doing
the same work in two industries, and only one of them is searching for both.

**Query both axes in every channel.** The method axis is usually where the roles nobody else
in your field is applying to turn out to be.

## Setting up alerts

1. Write your query, using the words the adverts use rather than the words you use.
2. Create it on two or three platforms, not all of them. **Three well-tuned alerts beat ten
   noisy ones**, because noisy alerts stop being read.
3. Re-tune after two weeks against what actually arrived. A query that returns nothing is as
   broken as one that returns everything.
4. Record the exact query, so you can compare and improve rather than re-inventing it.

## Reading a platform responsibly

**Stay inside each platform's terms of use, and browse at a human pace.** If a site asks you
to stop automating, stop. Do not automate anything behind a login, and do not collect data at
scale.

Contact-lookup services aggregate personal data from many sources. If you use one, use it for
a single identified person you have a genuine reason to contact, never for bulk outreach, and
check the local rules on unsolicited contact before you send anything.

**Automated bulk-applying is out of scope for this kit.** Most platforms' terms prohibit it,
and this kit's drafts-only rule exists precisely so that a person decides what gets sent.

## Verify before you invest

Two rules do more work than everything else on this page:

- **A posting is open only if you fetched it from the employer today.** See
  [G-10](../lessons/G-10-a-positive-test-before-any-claim.md).
- **A working link is not proof you may apply.** Read the eligibility clause first. See
  [G-12](../lessons/G-12-read-the-eligibility-clause-first.md).
